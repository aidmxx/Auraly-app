# Auraly AI prototype: article brief

I am writing an academic article about the interaction design of an Auraly AI reflective writing prototype. Please help me describe the prototype accurately, in clear academic English, as a four-step workflow. Distinguish design intentions from demonstrated outcomes, and do not invent participant data or claim that the prototype has been validated.

1. **Prompt Construction.** The user chooses one of four preset reflective prompts or writes a custom prompt. A custom prompt takes precedence over the selected preset. The user therefore helps define the topic and direction of the reflection before receiving AI support.
2. **Scaffolded Reflection.** The system presents five questions related to the chosen prompt. The questions support recall, feelings, influences, consequences, and learning; the user writes their own answers. At least one answer is needed to compose a draft. This stage is designed to elicit the user's ideas and experiences rather than provide a completed answer.
3. **Editable Final Reflection.** The system uses the prompt and the user's answers to generate a first-person draft. The user can review and edit the text before continuing. The intended role of AI is to help organize and articulate user-supplied material.
4. **Ownership Check.** The user rates how much the final reflection feels like their own work on a five-point scale, from “Not at all mine” to “Completely mine,” and may add an optional explanation. The rating is a self-report interaction in the prototype, not evidence that ownership increased.

The design question is whether involving users in prompt construction and the reflective writing process may support active contribution and perceived ownership of an AI-assisted final text. Describe this as a design rationale or research question, not an established causal finding. The attached screenshots are workflow illustrations from a demo, not participant data. The reconstructed app does not persist responses or ratings.
